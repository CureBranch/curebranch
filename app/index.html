How CureBranch works

What is this page?

This page explains CureBranch step by step, in plain English. Read it before you add any records. At the bottom is a short agreement.

Part 1: Learn about a cancer (anyone can do this)

  1. Open Cancer trees and pick a cancer. You can also search for it in the menu.
  2. Read the What is this page? box at the top. It tells you what the page shows.
  3. Press a ? button next to any word you don't know, like subtype or biomarker.
  4. Every number shows where it came from, like SEER or TCGA. Click the source to check it.
  5. If you use an AI chat tool, press Copy the prompt on the cancer page and paste it into your AI with your own records. More about using your AI

Part 2: Grow your own branch (only if you want to)

  1. Your records stay with you. You add them to this website on your phone or computer. They are saved only in this browser on this device. We have no server that keeps them.
  2. Records come from your hospital. You can connect your patient portal, or add a SMART Health Card or a FHIR file.
  3. You pick how much to share, from 4 levels. Private (device only), Basic, Detailed, or Full. Nothing is picked for you. Anything shared is shown under a random code like k7x2m9, never your name, birthday, street address or record numbers. Read about privacy
  4. Only checked records count. Records signed by a hospital or lab count in the tree's numbers. Everything else is shown but not counted. One person, one count.
  5. Your own AI, your own bill. If you want an AI to read your records, you use your own (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, Copilot or any other). CureBranch never runs AI on your data.
  6. You decide what happens later. You choose what happens to your summary after you die, and name a trusted person to carry it out.

The plain-English agreement

Check all 4 boxes to go on.

The step-by-step setup guide is in Settings, under Run the setup guide again. Still have questions? See Help.

    Privacy
    
      
    What is this page?
    This page explains, in plain English, what CureBranch keeps private, what you can choose to share, and what happens if you change your mind.
    
      
    The short version
    
        
      
          
    • Reading cancer pages needs no account and stores nothing about you.
      • 
          
    • Your records stay on your own device. We have no server that keeps them.
      • 
          
    • Nothing is shared until you pick a sharing level. Nothing is picked for you.
      • 
          
    • If you share, people see a random code, never your name, birthday, street or record numbers.
      • 
          
    • No ads. No tracking. No selling data.
      • 
        
    
      
    The 4 sharing levels
    
        
    What each sharing level shares
          
    LevelWhat is shared
    
          
            
    1. PrivateNothing. Everything stays on your device.
    
            
    2. BasicRanges only, like "Stage 3" and "age 60 to 69", your cancer type, markers and treatments.
    
            
    3. DetailedBasic, plus exact age and dates.
    
            
    4. FullDetailed, plus your city.
    
          
    
        
    You can turn off any part, like treatments, on My branch. Change your level any time in Settings.
    
      
    If you change your mind
    
        
    You can move to a lower level at any time. That stops sharing from then on. But anything already shared may have been copied by others. Only share what you would be OK with the world knowing someday.
    
      
    Translating and AI
    
        
    Translate on this device (in Chrome and Edge on a computer) keeps the words on your device. Browser translate in Chrome, Edge and Safari may send the page's words to Google, Microsoft or Apple. If you paste your records into an AI, that AI company's privacy rules apply. Leave out your name, birthday, address and record numbers.
    
      
    Deleting your data
    
        
    Go to Settings and press Delete everything on this device. Download a backup first if you want to keep a copy.
    
      
    Information, not medical advice. See also Help and Sources and licenses.
